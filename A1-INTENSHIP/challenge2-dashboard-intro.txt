CORRECTION:
1. Currency — correct

    You said GBP revenue should be £150. Correct.

    The $70 USD payment must not silently be added:

    £100 + £50 + $70
    because those aren't comparable units.

    A dashboard could expose:
        GBP Revenue: £150
        USD Revenue: $70

    Or, if the business wants a single reporting currency:

    Total Revenue: £X
    then we need an explicit FX conversion strategy.

    Never silently mix currencies.



2.) Refund — mostly correct, but there's a deeper issue

    You said: P004 was refunded, so it's not revenue generated that month.

    Good instinct, but we need to distinguish: gross revenue vs net revenue.

    Suppose:
        Successful payments: £10,000
        Refunds:              £2,000

    Then:
        Gross revenue = £10,000
        Net revenue   = £8,000

    So rather than pretending the original payment never existed, a serious financial system usually keeps the original transaction and records the refund separately.

    That gives us an audit trail.

    Payment
    £100 SUCCESSFUL
        │
        └──── Refund
                £100

    This is much safer than changing the original payment to somehow erase history.


3. Currency conversion — excellent

    You correctly identified the problem.

    We cannot do:

        Payment.objects.filter(
            status="SUCCESSFUL"
        ).aggregate(
            total=Sum("amount")
        )

    if multiple currencies exist.

    We need something like:

        Payment
        │
        ├── amount
        ├── currency
        └── status

    Then our reporting layer decides:
        "Give me GBP revenue"

    and either:
        GBP payments → use amount directly
        USD payments → convert USD → GBP
        EUR payments → convert EUR → GBP

    But here's your next professional-level consideration:
    Which exchange rate?

    The rate at:
        payment creation?
        payment settlement?
        end of day?
        report generation?

    You can't just call an exchange-rate API every time someone opens the dashboard and expect historical reports to remain stable.

    We'll come back to this.


4. Refund updates — you have the right instinct, but don't mutate history

    You said: it should automatically be updated real time with its current revenue state

    The dashboard should reflect the current state, yes.

    But don't think:

        Payment
        £100 SUCCESSFUL
                ↓
            Refund
                ↓
        Payment.amount = £0

        That's dangerous.

    Instead:

        Payment
        £100 SUCCESSFUL
                │
                └──── Refund
                    £100

    Then:

        Gross Revenue = £100
        Refunds       = £100
        Net Revenue   = £0

        The original financial event remains intact.

        That is auditability.


5. Your idempotency answer needs one important correction

You said:
    enforce uniqueness on transaction_id to avoid idempotency.

You're very close.
A unique constraint helps prevent duplicate records:

    transaction_id = models.CharField(
        max_length=255,
        unique=True,
    )

But uniqueness is not the entire concept of idempotency.

Imagine the payment provider sends:

    TX123 → SUCCESSFUL
    Our API receives it.

We create:
    Payment TX123
    SUCCESSFUL

Then the provider sends the exact same webhook again.

Our system should recognize:
    TX123 already processed
    and safely return success without creating another financial effect.
    That's idempotent processing.

The database constraint is an important safety net.
But the application logic also matters.

Now I want you to learn one of the most important concepts in payment systems

Imagine this happens:

Provider
   │
   │ "TX123 successful"
   ▼
Your Django API
   │
   ├── verify payment
   │
   ├── create payment
   │
   └── mark successful

But the provider sends the webhook three times:

TX123
TX123
TX123

Your backend must ensure:
    Financial effect = 1

not:
    Financial effect = 3

This is why we care about: Idempotency

The same operation can safely be performed multiple times without producing additional unintended effects.
For payments, this is absolutely fundamental.
